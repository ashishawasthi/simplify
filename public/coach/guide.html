1. What this is 🔗

Simplify is a free app for autistic children and teens: small tools for daily life, on a phone or a class iPad.

Simplify for coaches lets you write one simple page for your class: a picture story, the steps of a task, or class news. Your learners see it in My class, at the top of the app's menu, one screen at a time. It opens without internet too.

  • Learners never sign in and have no account. A device joins your class once, by scanning the class's QR code.
  • Learners see only the latest page you published. Your other pages stay drafts until you publish them.
  • You need a Google account and Chrome or Safari. A computer is easiest for writing; an iPad or phone works too.

What learners and their families read about My class: the learner guide's My class page.

2. Sign in with Google 🔗

  1. Open simplify.whiz.coach/coach/ in Chrome or Safari.
  2. Tap Sign in with Google, and choose your Google account in Google's own window.
  • Any Google account works: Gmail, or a Google account made with your school email. Simplify never sees your password.
  • Not inside WhatsApp or Telegram. A link opened from a chat opens in the chat app's own browser, and Google refuses to sign anyone in there. The page says so. Tap Copy the link, then paste it into Chrome or Safari.
  • Signing in lasts for this browser tab only. Closing the tab signs you out. On a shared computer or a class iPad, tap Sign out (top right) when you finish. On a computer your email shows beside it; on an iPad or phone, About you shows which account is signed in.
  • If the Google window doesn't open, allow pop-ups for this site, then try again.
  • Please turn on 2-Step Verification for your Google account, so nobody else can publish to your class.

3. About you, approval, and your classes 🔗

About you

The first time you sign in, fill in About you: your name, where you work, and how the admin can check who you are (your role, and a work email or phone number).

Under Where you work, tick each place where you coach, for example AWWA School @ Napiri — up to 10. The places you ticked show above the list; tap one to take it off. If your school or centre isn't in the list, type its name under the list, in Not in the list? The admin adds it to the list and puts it on your About you.

Only you and the admin see About you. Change it later from About you at the top. Signed up before Simplify asked where you work? About you opens first, once: tick your places and save.

Wait for the admin

Before you can make a class, write a page or add a picture, the admin checks who you are and where you work — for example, they may have seen you teach at your school, or they call it. Until then you see Waiting for approval. You can close the page: when the admin approves you, it changes to My classes by itself, or the next time you sign in.

  • If the admin does not approve you, you see Not approved, with what the admin wants you to know (if they wrote something). Change About you, then tap Ask the admin again: you are back to Waiting for approval.
  • Approved, but your school isn't in the list yet? You can make a class there once the admin has added it.
  • If you later change where you work, you stay approved. The admin sees that you changed it.

Make a class, or join one

On My classes, under Get a class:

  • New class: give its name, for example 3 Kindness (learners see the name when they join), choose where the class is — one of your places — and tap Make the class. It is ready at once, with a new random 9-character code such as K7M-3RQ-P9T, and it opens, ready for its first page. The class name is only a label.
  • Join a colleague's class: type its class code. Ask a coach of the class; the code is on the class's QR poster. Capitals, dashes and spaces don't matter. Tap Send to the admin. The request shows Waiting for the admin until the admin has checked that you really coach that class; then the class appears on My classes.

Every coach of a class can write, publish and change its pages.

4. Put the class on learners' devices 🔗

Open the class, then tap QR poster to print. Tap Print (beside the heading), or show it on a screen. It has the class name, the QR code, the class code in big letters, and three short steps for the adult setting up a device. Share it only with your class's families: anyone with the code can see what you publish.

Scan the QR code

  1. Point the learner's phone or iPad camera at the QR code, and open the link.
  2. Simplify asks Is this your class? with the class name. Tap Yes.

That is all. No account, no name, nothing to type.

Or type the code

No camera? At the bottom of Simplify's menu, tap ℹ️ How to use this app, then Set up this device, then Open set-up, and hold the button. My class is at the top: type the 9 letters and numbers. It finds the class as soon as the code is complete. The poster says this too.

iPad or iPhone with Simplify on the Home Screen: the camera opens the QR code in Safari, and Safari keeps its settings apart from the Home Screen app. So type the code inside the app: open Simplify from the Home Screen, tap How to use this app at the bottom, then Set up this device. Details: Add the class to this device and Set up this device.

  • Share the code only with your class's families. Anyone with the code can read what you publish (see section 6).
  • If the code gets out, make a new class: it gets a new code, and the devices are set up again with the new poster. The admin can then suspend the old class.
  • A school-managed iPad may block Google's servers. Ask the school to allow firestore.googleapis.com and firebasestorage.googleapis.com.

5. Write and publish the page 🔗

Open a class from My classes. On a computer the editor is on the left, and the preview (what learners see) and Publish are on the right.

  • Pages: the buttons at the top. Tap one to open it; + New page starts another. The page learners see now is marked Learners see this. Keep other pages as drafts for later.
  • Page name: for coaches only; learners don't see it.
  • Saving is automatic, a second after you stop typing: Saved Wed 8:05 am. With no internet it says Not saved yet; keep the page open and it saves when the internet is back.
  • Delete this page (under the text) deletes it; Undo on the message at the bottom brings it back.
  • Other coaches of the class see the same pages, and their changes as they happen.

Writing for your learners: one idea per screen; one to three short, literal sentences; the picture before the words; calm, and the same for teens as for children. The AI (section 9) writes this way too.

The preview

The phone-shaped Preview shows the page exactly as learners will see it, on the screen your cursor is on. Back and Next look through the rest. If the page uses a picture that isn't on the class's shelf, or a video not yet approved, a line under the phone says so: learners won't see it.

On a phone the editor comes first, then the preview:

Publish, Unpublish and Undo

  • Publish this page: learners now see this page. A message says Published. Learners see this page now. Its Undo puts back what learners saw before.
  • One page at a time. Learners see only the latest page published; publishing another page replaces it.
  • Changed a published page? Learners keep the old version until you tap Publish the changes. The line above the button says so: Learners see this page as it was when it was published.
  • The line above Publish always says what learners see, and since when. My classes shows the same for each class.
  • Unpublish: learners see nothing from you (Nothing from your coach yet). Undo publishes it again.
  • Deleting a page does not unpublish it: learners keep the published copy until you unpublish or publish another page.
  • Learners' devices pick up the change the next time they check, with internet: each time My class opens, and when the menu shows or the app comes back to the front (at most every 10 minutes).

6. What you publish is public 🔗

This warning is always beside the Publish button:

What you publish is public — anyone with the class code can see it. Never put pictures of students or any private or sensitive information in it.

Why

Learners never sign in, so the class code is the only key. Posters get photographed and codes get shared, so treat the page like a notice on the classroom door.

  • Never: learners' names or photos, faces, name tags, addresses, phone numbers, diagnoses, health or behaviour notes, or anything that identifies a learner or a family.
  • Fine: routines, steps for a task, class news without names, pictures of objects and places, hands doing a task.

You are the expert for your learners and decide what to publish; there is no consent box to tick. The AI also leaves names and private details out (section 9).

7. Pictures 🔗

Each class has a picture shelf, shared by its coaches. Pages can use only pictures on it.

  1. Add pictures (the Pictures panel under the editor). Choose one or more photos; on a phone or iPad you can take one with the camera.
  2. Under each picture, type a few words saying what it shows, for example Soap on my hands. They are saved as you type.
  3. Put the cursor where the picture should go in the page, then tap Put in page.

Or tap Picture in the toolbar, and tap a picture to put it in the page:

  • Made smaller on your device first: at most 1600 pixels on the long side, as a JPEG. The photo's location (GPS), camera and time details are removed before it uploads.
  • Why the words matter: screen readers read them to learners, and the AI uses them to choose the right picture for each screen.
  • A file that won't open (for example an iPhone HEIC photo on a Windows computer) says Try a JPEG or PNG photo.
  • Delete takes a picture off the shelf (Undo on the message). A page that used it then shows nothing there; the preview tells you.
  • Like the page, a picture on a published page can be seen by anyone with the class code. Never photos of students.

8. YouTube videos 🔗

  1. On YouTube, tap Share under the video, then Copy.
  2. In the editor, put the cursor where it should go and tap YouTube.
  3. Paste the link. ✓ A YouTube video means it is right.
  4. Type the words for the video's card, then tap Put it in the page. It goes on a line of its own.
  • Public and unlisted videos play; private ones don't. An unlisted video is a good way to share your own recording.
  • Learners see a card, Watch on YouTube (needs the internet). Nothing loads from YouTube until a learner taps it. Then the video plays inside the page, in YouTube's privacy-enhanced player.
  • YouTube can show ads (if the video's owner allows them), and suggest other videos from the same channel when it ends. Choose videos from channels you trust, and watch them to the end first.
  • After the tap, YouTube (Google) receives the device's internet address and which video was watched.
  • YouTube videos can't be watched offline.

9. Write with AI 🔗

Write with AI, just above the editor, writes or changes the page for you. On a new, empty page it is open; on a page that has words it is one line, so the editor stays in view: tap it to open it. Type what you want, then tap Ask the AI. It can take up to a minute.

What to type

  • Write a picture story about going to the dentist with my 4 pictures.
  • Make this simpler.
  • Put one idea on each screen.
  • Write the steps for buying a drink at the canteen, one step per screen.
  • Class news: sports day is on Friday 3 October. Wear your PE shirt and bring a water bottle.
  • Write a story about moving to a new classroom next Monday.
  • Add this video on the second screen: https://youtu.be/…

It sees what you type, the page in the editor now, the words of the class's pictures and approved videos (not the pictures themselves), and any links you give it.

It always starts with “I understood”

Every answer begins with I understood: …, the AI's own summary of what you asked. Read it: if it misunderstood, you see it at once. Then it does one of three things.

It writes a draft. The new page replaces the text in the editor just below, and is saved as a draft. Undo: bring back my text puts your own words back. Nothing reaches learners until you publish.

It asks questions (up to 3) when it needs to know more. Tap an answer, or type your own, then Send my answers.

It says no, kindly and in one line, when the request isn't about school, learning or daily life.

What it helps with, and what it doesn't

  • Yes: picture stories about routines and changes, steps for a task, class news, rules for a place, feelings and friendships, travel, money, food, festivals, safety.
  • Sensitive daily-living topics, yes, written factually and gently, with the correct words for body parts: puberty, periods, wet dreams, toileting, hygiene, body safety and consent, bullying, illness, death and grief, emergencies.
  • No: anything not for your learners or their class (personal errands, business or marketing, politics, adult content, writing for adults such as a speech, an essay or a job application, general chat); anything harmful or frightening for children; anything meant to shame, punish or threaten a learner.

How it writes

  • Short, literal sentences in plain Singapore English with British spelling; one idea per screen; a picture story of 3 to 8 screens, in the learner's own voice (I …).
  • No praise words, rewards, exclamation marks or emoji; suitable for teens too.
  • It never makes up facts such as a teacher's name, a time or a bus number. It asks, or writes in general words.
  • It uses only pictures on the class's shelf and links you gave it. When changing a page, it changes only what you asked.
  • It leaves out learners' names and private details (writing I or a student instead) and tells you in its note.

Always read the draft, and look through it in the preview, before you publish. The AI can get things wrong. You decide what learners see.

200 AI requests a month

  • Each coach has 200 AI requests a month (the admin sets the number). The panel shows how many you have used: AI requests: 12 of 200 this month.
  • Every answer counts, including questions and a no. Planning a short video counts too (section 10).
  • Not counted: an instruction too short to act on (the AI just asks what you want), and a request that failed (This request was not counted).
  • Months are Singapore months; the count starts again on the 1st. At the limit: You have used all 200 AI requests for this month.

10. Short videos 🔗

You can have AI make a short, realistic clip of one everyday action, such as hands washing at a sink, a card tapped at an MRT gate, or a tray returned at a hawker centre. It takes five steps, and each is your decision. Find them in the Videos panel under the editor.

  1. Plan. Open Make a short video, describe one calm scene, and tap Plan the video. This uses one AI request, not a video. Like Write with AI, the planner may ask questions, or say no.
  2. Check the exact request. You see The video request: the exact words the video maker will get, its length, and the words learners' screen readers will hear. Not right? Tap Change the description and plan again.
  3. Make. Tap Make the video. This uses 1 of your 5 videos this month; the line above the button says how many you have left. It takes about 2 minutes to start and a few more to finish. Keep working meanwhile; the video shows Being made, then updates by itself.
  4. Watch the private draft. When it says Ready to check, tap Watch it. Only the class's coaches can see it.
  5. Approve or discard. Approve puts it On the shelf; then Put in page (or the Video button in the toolbar) places it. Discard deletes it, and it still counts as one of your videos: it was already paid for.

What the videos are like

  • One calm, continuous, realistic shot, 3 to 10 seconds long (often about 8), landscape.
  • No text, captions, signs, logos or brands.
  • No real people or look-alikes: people are generic, faces stay out of close-up, and usually you see just hands and objects.
  • No music or voice; only soft, natural sounds of the action.
  • Places are generic Singapore settings (an HDB kitchen, a void deck, an MRT gate), never a named or recognisable real place.
  • The planner says no to bodies undressed (for puberty or toileting, use pictures and words instead), anything violent or frightening, real people or learners, cartoon characters, and anything that identifies a learner, family or school.
  • AI clips can look wrong in small ways (an odd hand, an object that changes). Watch the whole draft before you approve it.
  • If a video can't be made, it says Could not be made, and your video is given back. The same happens if it takes more than 30 minutes.
  • Learners' videos never play by themselves: a learner taps ▶. They are saved on the device for offline use.
  • At the limit: You have made all 5 videos for this month.

11. For the admin 🔗

The admin sees Admin at the top of the coach app, with a number when someone is waiting. Admins sign in with Google, like coaches, and are set up by the owner of Simplify in the database — never in the app. An admin approves their own About you like anyone else's.

The Admin screen has six tabs: Waiting, Coaches, Classes, Institutions, History and Limits.

Waiting: approving coaches

Everyone who has filled in About you and is waiting, with their name, email, where they work, how to check them, and when they asked. Check before approving: until you approve someone, they can't make classes, write pages, add pictures or use the AI.

  • Approve asks How did you check them? Write it down — at least 10 letters, for example Seen taking classes at AWWA School @ Napiri. It is kept in the history with your name and email; the coach doesn't see it. Then they can use Simplify at once: their own screen changes to My classes by itself.
  • Decline asks what they should know (if you like), for example Please add a work email I can check. The coach sees it, can change About you and ask again; they then show as Asked again.
  • Both have Undo on the message; Undo brings your note back.
  • Not in the list: the school the coach typed. Add it to the list fills in the name for you; add the organisation and save — it goes on their About you at once. If it is already in the list under another name, It's already listed lets you choose it.

Waiting: requests to join a class

A coach who works with a colleague asks to join the colleague's class. Each request shows the class, the coach, where they work, where the class is, its coaches, and their note. Approve only after checking they really coach it: Approve asks how you checked (for example Seen teaching 3 Kindness with Ms Tan), and they can work on that class at once. There is no Undo — take them off the class under Classes instead. Decline: Undo on the message.

Coaches

  • Each coach shows whether they are approved, waiting or not approved, and Approved by (or Declined by): which admin, when, and how they checked. Coaches approved before the history began say so. Changed …, after approval means they changed where they work after you approved them.
  • Suspend asks why (if you like, for the history): they can't change anything in any class, or use the AI or videos. Let back in undoes it.
  • History shows everything admins did about that coach. A coach you declined can be approved later.
  • More than 8 coaches: a search box finds one by name, email, school or status.

Classes

  • Take off beside a coach: they can no longer work on that class (for example, they moved school). Undo on the message.
  • Take the page down: learners see nothing from the class, at their device's next check. Undo on the message. A coach of the class can publish again, so if the coach's account is the problem, suspend the coach too.
  • Suspend the class: learners' devices stop showing its page, and its coaches can read but not change it. Make it active again undoes it.
  • A class code that got out: the coach makes a new class, then you suspend the old one once the devices have the new code.

Institutions

The places coaches choose from, grouped by organisation — one entry for each centre.

  • Add an institution: its name, organisation, and if you like its type and area.
  • Edit fixes a name or an area.
  • Retire a place that has closed: coaches no longer see it, but classes and coaches that chose it are not affected. Bring back undoes it.
  • When a coach has typed a school that isn't in the list, a note here says so and takes you to their card.

History

Every approval and change any admin makes, newest first: which admin (their name and Google email), what, when, and the note. Search it by a coach, an admin, a class or any word. Nobody can change or delete it from the app.

Limits

The same for every coach, counted in Singapore months: AI requests (Write with AI and video planning) and videos, each about US$0.80. Save the limits; Undo on the message.

Write with AI and videos use Google's Gemini models, which may run outside Singapore. They get what you type, the page and your pictures' words, never anything about learners. Simplify doesn't keep what you type to the AI; finished videos are stored in Singapore.

What learners' devices send

To get the page, a learner's device sends only the class code (and, like any website, its internet address). Nothing a learner types or taps is sent, and learners have no account. The device keeps a copy of the page, its pictures and its videos, so it works offline. When a learner taps a YouTube card, YouTube receives the device's address and which video is watched.

Deleting things

  • Pages: Delete this page. Pictures: Delete. Videos: Delete or Discard. Each has Undo for a few seconds, then it is gone.
  • Learners' devices drop an old page's pictures and videos at their next check with internet.
  • To delete your account and About you, or a whole class, ask the admin.

The coach app and this guide are kept out of search engines, and nothing learners see links here.
